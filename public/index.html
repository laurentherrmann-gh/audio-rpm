How it works

A 4-stroke engine fires each cylinder once every two crank turns, so its loudest tone is the firing frequency = rpm / 60 × cylinders / 2 (4 cylinders at 6,000 rpm → 200 Hz). Both detectors look for that tone between idle and redline and convert it back to rpm. Exactly half or double means a wrong lock: use Calibrate (type the in-game rpm while holding steady revs) or change the cylinder count.

Detection runs off the page: an audio worklet hands every chunk of sound straight to a worker, so a hidden tab or a busy page doesn't slow it. A reading describes the middle of its analysis window; with predict ahead the tracker extrapolates it to the newest sample. The adaptive pitch window shrinks to ~2.5 periods of the engine tone, so at high revs the window is a few milliseconds. Measured lag needs a known truth: use the simulated engine, or the loopback test to include the real capture path (BlackHole), then subtract the output latency shown below the table.
